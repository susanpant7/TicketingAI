## What multi-factor authentication is
Multi-factor authentication (MFA) asks for a second proof of identity, in addition to your password, when you sign in. It protects your account even if someone learns your password. MFA is mandatory for all employees for email, VPN and internal systems.

## Setting up MFA
Install the Microsoft Authenticator app on your phone. Then go to aka.ms/mfasetup on your laptop, choose "Add method", scan the QR code with the app, and approve the test prompt. Set it up in your first week, since your account will not work outside the office without it.

## Approving sign-in requests
When you sign in, a notification appears in the Authenticator app showing a two-digit number. Enter the number shown on your login screen into the app and approve. Approve only requests that you started yourself.

## Unexpected MFA prompts
If you receive an MFA prompt that you did not trigger, press "Deny" and report it to IT straight away. It may mean someone knows your password. Change your password at password.ticket.com after you report it.

## Getting a new phone
Before you wipe or replace your old phone, open the Authenticator app and enable backup in the settings, so your account moves to the new phone. If you already lost access to the old phone, contact IT support to reset your MFA, and re-register on the new phone. IT will verify your identity first.

## Lost or stolen phone
If your phone with Authenticator is lost or stolen, tell IT immediately so your sessions can be revoked. Use the self-service page or call IT, and register MFA again on the replacement phone. Also report the loss under the security incident process.

## Backup codes
You can generate 10 one-time backup codes in your security settings at aka.ms/mfasetup. Store them in a safe place away from your phone. Each code works once, and you can use one when you cannot reach your phone.
