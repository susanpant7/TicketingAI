# Social Security Fund and Gratuity

## What the Social Security Fund is
The Social Security Fund (SSF) is Nepal's government-run fund for the social protection of employees. The company enrols every employee in SSF from the first month of employment. Contributions are deducted from your salary and matched by the company, and the company deposits both parts to SSF every month.

## Contribution rates
The employee contributes 11 percent of basic salary, and the company contributes 20 percent of basic salary. The total of 31 percent goes into your SSF account. The employee's 11 percent is deducted from your salary, and the payslip shows the amount under SSF.

## What SSF gives you
SSF covers several schemes: medical, health and maternity benefits, accident and disability benefits, a benefit for your dependent family if you die, and an old-age (retirement) benefit. The company's share also covers your gratuity, so there is no separate gratuity payment at the end of your service.

## Registering with SSF
HR registers you with SSF during onboarding. You will need your citizenship certificate and PAN, and a recent photograph. If you already have an SSF number from a previous employer, give it to HR so your records are linked, and do not apply for a new one.

## Checking your balance
You can check your contributions and balance through your SSF account on the SSF portal, using the number HR gives you. Contributions appear after the company deposits them each month. Tell payroll at payroll@ticket.com if a month is missing.

## Leaving the company
Tell HR when you resign so that the contribution record is closed correctly. HR gives you a clearance letter that you may need to claim or transfer your balance. The rules for withdrawal are set by SSF, and HR can point you to the right office.

## Other savings options
The Citizen Investment Trust (CIT) and other retirement schemes are personal options outside the company plan. Contributions you make to CIT can be declared to payroll for tax deductions within the limits of the current tax rules.
